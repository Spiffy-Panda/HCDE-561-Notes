HCDE 561: Strategic Human-Centered AI

**Personal Context File — Interview Prompt Sheet**

Use this when you’re the interviewer. Your goal is to pull specific, concrete, voice-carrying details out of your partner — not to complete every question perfectly.

## How to use this sheet

- You have roughly 15 minutes. Don’t try to ask everything. Pick the questions that feel live and follow them.
- **Write as they talk.** You’re drafting their context file, not taking notes. Write in their voice, using their words.
- **Follow the specifics.** If they say “I write a lot of internal memos,” your follow-up is “what’s a typical one?” or “who reads them?” or “what do you wish they were shorter or longer?”
- **Push past the generic first answer.** The first pass is usually a job-interview answer. The good stuff is in the second or third layer.
Sample follow-ups that usually work:

- *“Tell me about a specific one.”*
- *“What would be wrong in that one?”*
- *“Who’s the audience for that?”*
- *“What would you never do?”*

## Section 1 — Role and current work (3-4 min)

1. What’s your job title? What does it actually mean day-to-day — what do you spend your time on?
1. What’s one project you’re in the middle of right now? What’s your role on it?
1. Who’s your audience most of the time? (Stakeholders, clients, colleagues, leadership, etc. — get specific.)
1. What kind of organization are you in? Size, sector, culture in one sentence.

## Section 2 — Voice and communication (3-4 min)

1. What kinds of things are you writing all the time? (Emails, memos, presentations, research, code, spec docs, slides, proposals, etc.)
1. If I showed you two versions of the same email — one that sounds like you and one that sounds AI-generated — how would I know which was which? What’s the tell?
1. Are there words, phrases, or formats you find yourself removing or rewriting constantly? Any “never use these words” rules?
1. How formal or informal is your default voice? Does it shift by audience?
*Interviewer note: after this section, ask them to say 2-3 sentences you can capture verbatim as voice samples. “Tell me what you did yesterday at work, in your own words.” Write down what they say word-for-word.*

## Section 3 — Standards and anti-patterns (2-3 min)

1. What do you hold yourself to that maybe others don’t? (Quality bar, process, rigor, etc.)
1. What would be embarrassing to ship? What’s the failure mode you worry about?
1. Are there things you’d never do at work, that you want an AI helping you to also never do?

## Section 4 — What AI should and shouldn’t touch (2-3 min)

1. What’s a task you’d hand off to an AI tomorrow, if it worked well enough?
1. What’s a task you would never hand off? Why not?
1. Where in your work does your voice matter most? Where does it matter least?

## Section 5 — People and collaborators (1-2 min)

1. Who do you write for or work with most often? (Roles, relationships, dynamics. First names are fine — or just roles.)
1. Is there anyone whose style or approach you explicitly don’t want to mimic?

## Section 6 — Expertise and non-expertise (1-2 min)

1. What are you actually an expert in? (Not your job title — the real thing you know deeply.)
1. What are you not an expert in, but people sometimes assume you are? (Important — keeps the AI from overclaiming in your weak areas.)

## Section 7 — Anything else (1 min)

1. Is there anything that would surprise someone new to working with you? A quirk, a pet peeve, a preference, a ritual?
1. What’s the one thing you’d want an AI tool to know about you that nothing so far has captured?

## When you’re done

- Read back what you wrote. Let them correct it.
- Mark any spots where the answer was thin or where they couldn’t easily answer — those are spots to flag for homework revision.
- Hand them the draft. They own it now.
